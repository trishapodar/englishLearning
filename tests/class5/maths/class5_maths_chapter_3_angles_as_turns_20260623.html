Section A

Q1. acute angle
Marking Scheme / Steps: Recall the definition of angles based on turns from the text.
A quarter (1/4) turn is a right angle.
A turn that is less than a quarter (1/4) turn is defined as an acute angle.
Q2. 1/2 turn (half turn)
Marking Scheme / Steps: A single quarter turn is represented as the fraction 1/4 of a full turn.
Taking two quarter turns in the same direction is calculated as: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 of a turn.
Simplifying the fraction 2/4 by dividing the numerator and denominator by 2 gives 1/2 turn, which is a half turn.
Q3. A half turn
Marking Scheme / Steps: A single quarter turn is represented as 1/4 of a complete turn.
Making 2 quarter turns in the same direction is calculated as: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 of a turn.
Simplifying the fraction 2/4 gives 1/2, which represents a half turn.
Q4. 1/2 turn
Marking Scheme / Steps: A single quarter turn is equal to 1/4 of a full turn.
Taking two quarter turns in the same direction is calculated as: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 turn.
Simplifying 2/4 gives 1/2 turn (which is also known as a half turn or a straight angle).
Q5. South
Marking Scheme / Steps: One right angle (90°) turn clockwise from North points to East.
A second right angle (90°) turn clockwise from East points to South.
Therefore, turning by two right angles (2 × 90° = 180°) clockwise from North results in facing South.
Q6. 3/8 turn, forming an obtuse angle
Marking Scheme / Steps: Convert the second turn to a fraction with a common denominator: 1/4 = 2/8 turn.
Add the two turns together to find the total turn: 1/8 + 2/8 = 3/8 turn.
Compare the total turn to a quarter turn (1/4 or 2/8) and a half turn (1/2 or 4/8). Since 3/8 is greater than a quarter turn but less than a half turn, it forms an obtuse angle.
Q7. Anti-clockwise movement
Marking Scheme / Steps: Recall the definition of clock hand movements from the text: the direction in which the hands of a clock move is called clockwise movement.
Identify that the movement in the opposite direction is defined as anti-clockwise movement.
Therefore, the correct option is Anti-clockwise movement.
Q8. half turn
Marking Scheme / Steps: A single quarter turn is represented by the fraction 1/4 of a full turn.
Taking two quarter turns in the same direction means combining them: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 of a turn.
Simplifying the fraction 2/4 gives 1/2, which is equal to a half turn.
Q9. 10 minutes, pointing to 2
Marking Scheme / Steps: A full turn of the minute hand around the clock face represents 60 minutes.
To find the minutes passed in a 1/6 turn, we calculate: 1/6 × 60 minutes = 10 minutes.
Starting from 12 and moving in a clockwise direction, 5 minutes corresponds to the number 1, and 10 minutes corresponds to the number 2.
Thus, the minute hand has moved by 10 minutes and points to 2.
Q10. An obtuse angle
Marking Scheme / Steps: Recall the definitions of angles based on turns from the textbook.
A quarter (1/4) turn is a right angle.
A turn less than a quarter (1/4) turn is an acute angle.
A turn that is more than a quarter (1/4) turn but less than a half (1/2) turn is defined as an obtuse angle.

Q3.
1. The total fraction of a full turn is 1/3 (or 4/12). 2. The minute hand points to the number 4 on the clock face. 3. A total of 20 minutes have passed.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Calculate the total turn: 1/4 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3 turn. [1 Mark]
Determine the final position: Since a full clock face has 12 divisions, a 4/12 turn means the hand points to the number 4. [1 Mark]
Calculate the total minutes: 1/3 of 60 minutes = 20 minutes (or 15 minutes for the first turn + 5 minutes for the second turn = 20 minutes). [1 Mark]
Q4.
(a) Taking 2 quarter turns is equal to 2 × 1/4 = 1/2 turn, which is a half turn. This represents a straight angle. (b) Taking 4 quarter turns is equal to 4 × 1/4 = 1 turn, which completes exactly 1 full turn.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Award 1 mark for correctly calculating that 2 quarter turns equal a 1/2 turn (or half turn).
Award 1 mark for identifying that a half turn represents a straight angle.
Award 1 mark for correctly calculating that 4 quarter turns equal 1 full turn.
Q5.
1. To find the first total turn, we add the two fractions by finding a common denominator: 1/4 + 1/8 = 2/8 + 1/8 = 3/8 turn

Adding another 1/8 turn to the previous total: 3/8 + 1/8 = 4/8 turn

Simplifying 4/8 by dividing the numerator and denominator by 4 gives: 4 ÷ 4/8 ÷ 4 = 1/2 turn

The final total turn is a 1/2 turn (or a half turn).
Marking Scheme / Steps: [1 Mark] Correctly converts 1/4 to 2/8 and adds it to 1/8 to get the first total of 3/8 turn.
[1 Mark] Correctly adds the second 1/8 turn to get 4/8 turn.
