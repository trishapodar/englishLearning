SECTION C — 3 Mark(s) Each

Q9.   Digestion Process (Mouth to Stomach)   [3 Mark(s)]

1. Mouth: The teeth chew and grind the food. Saliva mixes with it to make it soft and starts digesting carbohydrates.

2. Food Pipe (Oesophagus): The swallowed food travels down a tube called the food pipe to reach the stomach.

3. Stomach: In the stomach, food is churned and mixed with digestive juices that further break it down.

Marking: 1 mark for each step correctly explained (Mouth, Food pipe, Stomach).
Accept: Mentioning specific parts and their basic role.
Common Error: Students skip the food pipe and jump directly from mouth to stomach.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science, Ch. Tasting to Digesting

Q10.   Food Preservation Methods   [3 Mark(s)]

1. Drying (Sun drying): Removing water from food. Example: Drying grapes to make raisins, or making papad.

2. Pickling/Salting: Adding excess salt and oil. Example: Making mango or lemon pickles.

3. Refrigeration (Cooling): Keeping food at a low temperature to stop germs from growing. Example: Keeping milk or cooked vegetables in the fridge.

Marking: 1 mark for each method with an appropriate example.
Accept: Adding sugar (jams) / Boiling (milk) / Canning.
Common Error: Students give the method but forget to provide an example.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science, Ch. Mangoes Round the Year

Q11.   Proteins and Body-building   [3 Mark(s)]

Proteins are essential nutrients required by our body. They are called 'body-building foods' because they help our body to grow and help in repairing damaged parts (like when we get hurt). Two food items rich in proteins are Milk and Eggs (or pulses/soyabean).

Marking: 1 mark for what proteins do (growth/repair). 1 mark for the 'body-building' explanation. 1 mark for two correct examples.
Accept: Meat, fish, lentils, beans for examples.
Common Error: Students often list energy-giving foods (rice/wheat) as protein sources.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science

Q12.   Malnutrition and Junk Food   [3 Mark(s)]

Under-nutrition happens when a person does not get enough food or the right nutrients, making them weak and sick.

Obesity happens when a person eats too much fat-rich food, leading to excessive weight gain.

Eating too much junk food affects our health because it contains high fats and sugars but lacks essential vitamins, minerals, and roughage, leading to obesity and dullness.

Marking: 1 mark for Under-nutrition definition. 1 mark for Obesity definition. 1 mark for the effect of junk food.
Accept: Junk food makes us lazy / leads to diseases like diabetes.
Common Error: Students focus only on obesity and forget to explain under-nutrition.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science

Q13.   Signs of Food Spoilage   [3 Mark(s)]

We can identify spoiled food through three main signs:

1. Foul Smell: The food gives off a bad or sour odor.

2. Change in Color or Appearance: The food may turn black, green, or have white fuzzy mould growing on it.

3. Change in Taste: If tasted (which should be avoided if suspected spoiled), it tastes sour, bitter, or stale.

Marking: 1 mark for each valid sign (Smell, Color/Appearance, Taste).
Accept: Texture changes (becomes slimy or mushy).
Common Error: Students write 'when flies sit on it' which is a cause of contamination, not a sign of the food itself being already spoiled.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science, Ch. Mangoes Round the Year

SECTION D — 5 Mark(s) Each

Q16.   Complete Digestion Process   [5 Mark(s)]

The process of digestion involves several steps:

1. Mouth: Digestion begins here where teeth chew the food and saliva softens it.

2. Food Pipe: Pushes the swallowed food down into the stomach.

3. Stomach: Churns the food and mixes it with digestive juices, turning it into a paste.

4. Small Intestine: This is where the final digestion takes place. Liver and pancreas pour their juices here. Role: The walls of the small intestine absorb the useful nutrients from the digested food into the blood.

5. Large Intestine: Role: It receives the undigested food. It absorbs excess water from it, forming solid waste (feces), which is eventually thrown out through the anus.

Marking: 3 marks for describing the flow (Mouth to Stomach). 1 mark for the role of the Small Intestine. 1 mark for the role of the Large Intestine.
Accept: Mentioning nutrient absorption and water absorption clearly.
Common Error: Students confuse the roles of the small and large intestines.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science, Ch. Tasting to Digesting

Q17.   Balanced Diet and Example   [5 Mark(s)]

Concept: A balanced diet is a diet that contains all the necessary nutrients—carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals—along with water and roughage in the correct proportions required by our body.

Importance: It is essential for children because they are in their growing age. They need proteins for growth, carbohydrates for energy to study and play, and vitamins/minerals to protect them from diseases.

Sample Lunch Meal:

→ Carbohydrates: Chapati or Rice

→ Proteins: Dal (Lentils) or Paneer/Chicken

→ Vitamins/Minerals & Roughage: A bowl of mixed vegetable salad (cucumber, carrots) and cooked spinach.

→ Calcium/Fats: A small bowl of curd/yogurt or a spoon of ghee on chapati.

Marking: 2 marks for the correct definition of a balanced diet. 1.5 marks for explaining its importance for children. 1.5 marks for providing a realistic and complete sample meal.
Accept: Any culturally relevant meal that includes major nutrient groups.
Common Error: Students list a meal consisting only of fast food or missing a major nutrient group like proteins or vegetables.

Ref: NCERT Class 5 Environmental Studies/Science
