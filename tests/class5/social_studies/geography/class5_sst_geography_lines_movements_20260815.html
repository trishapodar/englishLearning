Section C

Q7. Map Reading and Scale
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Marks: (i) Purpose of Map Scale: The Earth is too large to draw at its actual size on paper. A map scale reduces large real-world distances proportionately so that accurate ground distances can be measured easily. [1 Mark]
(ii) Distance Calculation: Given scale: 1 cm = 50 km. Distance on map = 6 cm. Actual distance = 6 × 50 km = 300 km. Answer: 300 km [1 Mark]
Q8. Rotation vs Revolution
Model Answer & Differentiation: (i) Time Taken: Rotation - 24 hours (1 Day) [0.5 Mark] | Revolution - 365 days and 6 hours (1 Year) [0.5 Mark]
(ii) Main Effect: Rotation - Causes Day and Night [0.5 Mark] | Revolution - Causes Change of Seasons [0.5 Mark]
Q9. Geographic Grid
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Marks: (a) Definition: The network formed by the criss-crossing of horizontal lines of latitude and vertical lines of longitude on a globe or map is called the Geographic Grid. [1 Mark]
(b) Practical Application: The point where a line of latitude intersects a line of longitude gives the exact coordinates (address) of any location on Earth, enabling pilots, ships, and GPS applications to pinpoint exact locations. [1 Mark]

Section D

Q10. Long Answer Question
Model Answers & Rubric: Part (a) Leap Year Calculation [2 Marks]:
- The Earth takes 365 days and 6 hours (1/4th of a day) to complete one full revolution around the Sun. [0.5 Mark]
- For convenience, a normal calendar year is counted as 365 days, and the remaining 6 hours are saved each year. [0.5 Mark]
- In four years, these extra hours add up: 6 hours × 4 = 24 hours = 1 full day. [0.5 Mark]
- This extra day is added to February every 4th year (making February 29 days long and the year 366 days). [0.5 Mark]

Part (b) Summer Solstice [2 Marks]:
- Date: It occurs on June 21. [0.5 Mark]
- Phenomenon: The Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, and direct sun rays fall on the Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N). [0.5 Mark]
- Effect in Northern Hemisphere: It brings summer, and this day has the longest day and the shortest night of the year. [1 Mark]

Part (c) Opposite Seasons in Australia [1 Mark]:
- Australia is in the Southern Hemisphere. When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun (experiencing winter in December), the Southern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, receiving direct sunlight and experiencing Summer. [1 Mark]
