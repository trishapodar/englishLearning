SECTION A: Objective & Analytical Questions

Q1. (b) Formation of Karst topography through solution and chemical weathering
Marking Scheme / Steps: Endogenic forces originate inside the Earth (tectonics), whereas exogenic forces (water, wind, ice) act on the surface. Karst topography is formed by groundwater dissolving soluble limestone—an exogenic chemical weathering/erosion process.
Q2. (b) Decrease in gradient and loss of energy leading to lateral erosion and deposition
Marking Scheme / Steps: As slope gradient flattens in the middle course, river velocity decreases, shifting the work from vertical bed incision to lateral erosion and sediment deposition on bends.
Q3. (c) Weathering is a static / in-situ breakdown of rocks; erosion involves dynamic transportation.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Weathering is non-motional in-situ disintegration; erosion requires mobile transportation agents like running water, wind, or glaciers.
Q4. (b) Sea cave → Sea arch → Collapse of arch roof → Sea stack
Marking Scheme / Steps: Waves erode headlands into sea caves → caves cut through to form arches → roof of arch collapses under gravity → leaves isolated rock pillar called a sea stack.
Q5. (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Marking Scheme / Steps: The Ring of Fire boundaries coincide with subduction zones around the Pacific plate, directly causing ~75% of active volcanoes and major earthquakes.
Q6. (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Trees stabilize slope material; removing vegetation reduces root binding power, making slopes hyper-susceptible to shear failure during heavy rain.
Q7. Karst Topography
Marking Scheme / Steps: [0.5 Mark] Definition: Karst Topography refers to a landscape created by groundwater dissolving soluble carbonate rocks such as limestone, dolomite, or gypsum.
[0.5 Mark] Feature: Sinkholes / Stalactites / Stalagmites / Underground Cave Rivers / Doline.
Q8. Contour Continuous Trenching (CCT)
Marking Scheme / Steps: [0.5 Mark] Definition: Contour Continuous Trenching (CCT) involves excavating continuous horizontal trenches along the contour lines of a slope.
[0.5 Mark] Mechanism: It breaks the speed of surface runoff, traps eroding topsoil, increases rainwater infiltration, and prevents deep rill/gully formation.

SECTION B: Short Answer Questions - SAQ I

Q9. Weathering Types
Marking Scheme / Steps: Physical / Mechanical Weathering (1 Mark): Physical breakdown of rocks into smaller fragments without altering chemical composition. Mechanism: Frost wedging (water freezing in rock cracks expanding by ~9%), temperature fluctuation, or salt crystallization.
Chemical Weathering (1 Mark): Decomposition of rock minerals due to chemical reactions changing their chemical structure. Mechanism: Carbonation (acidic rain dissolving limestone), Oxidation (rusting of iron-rich rocks), or Hydration.
Biological Weathering (1 Mark): Disintegration caused by living organisms. Mechanism: Plant roots growing into rock joints wedging them apart, burrowing animals, or organic acids secreted by lichens/mosses.
Q10. Glacial Action
Marking Scheme / Steps: Glacial Moraines & U-shaped Valley Formation (1.5 Marks):
U-shaped Valley: As a mountain glacier moves downhill under gravity, its immense weight plucks and gouges both the bottom and side walls of a pre-existing V-shaped river valley, carving steep sides and a wide, flat bottom. (0.75 Mark)
Moraines: Accumulations of unsorted rock debris, boulders, sand, and till deposited by melting glaciers along their edges (lateral), center (medial), or terminus (terminal). (0.75 Mark)
Comparison with Mountain River (1.5 Marks):
River erosion creates sharp V-shaped valleys through concentrated vertical water incision and bed load grinding. (0.75 Mark)
Glacial erosion creates wide U-shaped valleys through broad plucking and abrasion across the entire valley floor and sides. (0.75 Mark)

SECTION C: Short Answer Questions - SAQ II

Q11. Soil Erosion Impacts
Marking Scheme / Steps: (a) Three Impacts of Soil Erosion (3 Marks - 1 Mark per point):
Agricultural Degradation: Removal of nutrient-rich topsoil layer (humus) leads to loss of land fertility, declining crop yields, and desertification.
Settlement & Infrastructure Displacement: Coastal erosion destroys homes, roads, and sea walls, forcing coastal communities to relocate.
Hydrological Silting: Eroded sediment silts up river beds and reservoirs, reducing water storage capacity and drastically increasing downstream flood frequencies.
(b) Two Mitigation Interventions (1 Mark - 0.5 Mark each):
Check Dams / Earthen Bunds: Built across stream channels to reduce runoff velocity and trap sediment.
Shelterbelts / Contour Farming / Afforestation: Planting rows of trees perpendicular to wind direction or ploughing along contour lines to hold soil intact.
Q12. Life Cycle of a River
Marking Scheme / Steps: Upper Course (1.5 Marks): High altitude, steep gradient, fast water velocity. Dominant process is vertical erosion (incision). Formations: Deep V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, and waterfalls (where hard rock cap overlies soft rock).
Middle Course (1.5 Marks): Moderate gradient, increased volume. Dominant process shifts to lateral erosion and transportation. Formations: Meanders, oxbow lakes (cut-off meander loops), and broad floodplains.
Lower Course & Mouth (1 Mark): Flat gradient, low velocity. Dominant process is deposition. Sediment load drops at the river mouth entering the sea, forming a triangular deposit of alluvium broken by distributaries called a Delta.

SECTION D: Case-Based / Source-Based Integrated Question

Q13. Seismic Activity and Mud Volcanoes
Marking Scheme / Steps: (i) Ancient vs Modern View of Earthquakes (2 Marks):
Ancient View: Attributed tremors to supernatural or macroscopic elemental interactions and observed external signals like animal distress and water shifts. (1 Mark)
Modern View: Grounded in lithospheric plate tectonics where stress build-up along crustal fault zones leads to sudden elastic strain release, producing seismic shockwaves. (1 Mark)
(ii) Baratang Mud Volcano vs Igneous Volcano (2 Marks):
Baratang Mud Volcano: Non-igneous eruption driven by subterranean trapped gases (methane) and underground water pressurizing soft sediment, extruding cold clay/mud slurry. (1 Mark)
Igneous Volcano: Driven by high-temperature mantle magma, emitting red-hot molten lava, volcanic ash, and pyroclastic flows at temperatures >1000°C. (1 Mark)
(iii) Boundary Type & Mechanism (2 Marks):
Boundary Type: Transform / Strike-Slip Boundary (e.g., San Andreas Fault). (1 Mark)
Mechanical Interaction: Two tectonic plates slide horizontally past each other. Friction locks the plates until shear stress exceeds rock strength, causing a sudden jerk release (earthquake) without creating or destroying crustal material. (1 Mark)

SECTION E: Long Answer Questions

Q14. Plate Tectonics OR Mountains Formation
Marking Scheme / Steps: (A) Plate Tectonics
Introduction (1 Mark): Define Tectonic Plates.
1. Convergent Boundary (1.75 Marks): Motion: Toward each other. Force: Compressional stress. Landforms: Fold Mountains, oceanic trenches, volcanic island arcs.
2. Divergent Boundary (1.75 Marks): Motion: Pull away. Force: Tensional stress. Landforms: Mid-Oceanic Ridges, Continental Rift Valleys.
3. Transform Boundary (1.5 Marks): Motion: Slide horizontally. Force: Shear stress / Friction. Landforms: Fault lines (frequent earthquakes).

--- OR ---

(B) Fold vs Block Mountains
Fold Mountains (3 Marks): Force: Compressional forces. Structure: Anticlines (upfolds) and Synclines (downfolds). Example: Himalayas / Alps / Rockies.
Block Mountains (3 Marks): Force: Tensional or compressional forces causing faulting. Structure: Horsts (Block Mountains) and Grabens (Rift Valleys). Example: Vosges Mountains / Black Forest / Vindhya & Satpura ranges.
Q15. Mountain and Coastal Disasters
Marking Scheme / Steps: 1. Definitions (2 Marks): GLOF: Sudden release of enormous volumes of water held in a glacial lake due to dam breach. Avalanche: Rapid downhill fall of a massive body of snow, ice, and rock debris.
2. Triggers (2 Marks): Natural: Heavy rainfall, rapid snowmelt, seismic shaking. Anthropogenic: Deforestation, heavy machinery blasting, climate change.
3. Three-Tier Mitigation Strategy (2 Marks): Structural: Siphoning water, deflection walls. Early Warning Systems (EWS): Automated weather stations, sensors. Community Preparedness: Mapping hazard zones, evacuation drills.
