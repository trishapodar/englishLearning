Section A

Q1. (b) The iron block has more inertia because it has greater mass.
Explanation: Inertia is the natural tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or uniform motion. Mass is the direct quantitative measure of inertia. Since iron has a much higher density and therefore greater mass than the wooden block of identical volume, the iron block has greater inertia.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for correct option (b) with mention of mass.
Q2. (c) Be doubled
Explanation: From Newton's Second Law, F = m × a, which gives a = F / m. If force F is doubled while mass m remains constant, the acceleration a also doubles.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for correct option (c).
Q3. (b) Act on two different bodies in opposite directions
Explanation: Newton's Third Law states that action and reaction forces always act on two different interacting bodies in opposite directions simultaneously.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for correct option (b).
Q4. (a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Explanation: Both statements are true. By pulling the hands backward, the fielder increases the contact time t, which decreases the rate of change of momentum (Δp / t). Since force F equals the rate of change of momentum, the force exerted on the palms is significantly reduced.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for correct option (a).

Section B

Q5. Balanced vs Unbalanced Forces
Model Answer: (a) Difference: Balanced Forces: The net resultant of all forces acting on the body is zero (F_net = 0). They cannot change the state of rest or uniform motion of a body (no acceleration). [0.5 Mark] | Unbalanced Forces: The net resultant force is non-zero (F_net ≠ 0). They cause acceleration, changing the speed or direction of motion of the body. [0.5 Mark]
(b) Example of Balanced Forces: A heavy book resting on a table (downward gravitational force balanced by the upward normal reaction force from the table), or a tug-of-war game where neither team moves because both pull with equal force. [1 Mark]
Q6. Acceleration and Braking Force
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Marks: Given Data: Mass of car (m) = 1200 kg. Initial velocity (u) = 72 km/h. Final velocity (v) = 0 m/s. Time taken (t) = 5 s.
(i) Unit Conversion: u = 72 × (5 / 18) = 20 m/s [0.5 Mark]
(ii) Acceleration & Braking Force Calculation: Using the first equation of motion: a = (v - u) / t = (0 - 20) / 5 = -4 m/s² [0.5 Mark]. Using Newton's Second Law: F = m × a = 1200 kg × (-4 m/s²) = -4800 N [0.5 Mark]. Magnitude of the braking force = 4800 N (The negative sign indicates retarding force). [0.5 Mark]

Section C

Q7. Newton's Second Law & Derivation
Model Answer & Derivation: (a) Statement: The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts. [1 Mark]
(b) Mathematical Derivation: Let an object of mass m have initial velocity u and final velocity v after time t. Initial momentum, p1 = m × u [0.5 Mark]. Final momentum, p2 = m × v. Change in momentum, Δp = p2 - p1 = m(v - u) [0.5 Mark]. Rate of change of momentum = m(v - u) / t. According to the law: F ∝ m(v - u) / t. Since acceleration a = (v - u) / t: F ∝ m × a. F = k × m × a [0.5 Mark]. In SI units, 1 unit of force is defined such that constant k = 1. Therefore: F = m × a [0.5 Mark]
Q8. Conservation of Momentum & Recoil Velocity
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Marks: (a) Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum: In an isolated system with no external unbalanced force, the total linear momentum of the interacting bodies remains constant (conserved) before and after interaction. [1 Mark]
(b) Recoil Velocity Calculation: Given Data: Mass of bullet (m1) = 20 g = 0.02 kg. Velocity of bullet (v1) = 150 m/s. Mass of rifle (m2) = 4 kg. Initial velocities (u1 = u2) = 0 m/s (at rest before firing). Recoil velocity of rifle = v2.
Applying Conservation of Momentum: Total momentum before firing = m1 × u1 + m2 × u2 = 0 [0.5 Mark]. Total momentum after firing = m1 × v1 + m2 × v2. 0 = (0.02 kg × 150 m/s) + (4 kg × v2) [0.5 Mark]. 0 = 3 kg·m/s + 4 × v2. 4 × v2 = -3. v2 = -3 / 4 = -0.75 m/s [0.5 Mark].
Final Answer: The recoil velocity of the rifle is 0.75 m/s in the direction opposite to the bullet's motion. [0.5 Mark]

Section D

Q9. Case-Based Model Answers
Case-Based Model Answers: Part (a) [1.5 Marks]: Property: Inertia of Motion. [0.5 Mark]. Definition/Explanation: It is the inherent property of a body by virtue of which it resists any change in its state of uniform motion. When brakes are applied, the lower part of the passenger's body in contact with the car comes to rest immediately, while the upper part continues moving forward due to inertia of motion. [1 Mark]

Part (b) [1.5 Marks]: Mechanism: From F = Δp / Δt, force is inversely proportional to the time taken for momentum to reduce to zero. [0.5 Mark]. The airbag cushions the passenger, significantly increasing the time duration (Δt) over which the momentum of the head and torso becomes zero. [0.5 Mark]. As time increases, the rate of change of momentum drops, drastically reducing the impact force on the passenger and preventing fatal injuries. [0.5 Mark]

Part (c)(i) Momentum Calculation [1.5 Marks]: Formula: Momentum (p) = Mass (m) × Velocity (v). Delivery Van: p_van = 2000 kg × 20 m/s = 40000 kg·m/s [0.5 Mark]. Compact Car: p_car = 800 kg × 20 m/s = 16000 kg·m/s [0.5 Mark]. Conclusion: The delivery van has significantly greater momentum (40000 kg·m/s > 16000 kg·m/s) due to its larger mass. [0.5 Mark]

Part (c)(ii) Stopping Time Calculation [1.5 Marks]: Formula: From Newton's Second Law: Braking Force (F) = Initial Momentum / Stopping Time (t). t = p / F [0.5 Mark]. Time for Delivery Van: t_van = 40000 kg·m/s / 4000 N = 10 seconds [0.5 Mark]. Time for Compact Car: t_car = 16000 kg·m/s / 4000 N = 4 seconds. Conclusion: The delivery van takes longer to come to a complete stop (10 s compared to 4 s for the car) because of its larger initial momentum against the same braking force. [0.5 Mark]
