SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (8 MARKS)

Q3. Krishtakka's Determination
Key Value Points:
When the narrator was away at a wedding, Krishtakka was unable to read the next instalment of the serialized novel Kashi Yatre in the magazine Karmaveera. She felt helpless, illiterate, and dependent on others to know the story. This made her realize that dependency is a sorrow, inspiring her resolution to learn to read Kannada independently before Saraswati Puja (Dussehra).
Marking Breakdown: [1 Mark] Content. [1 Mark] Grammar and expression.
Q4. Refrain in "Bharat Our Land"
Key Value Points:
"She’s peerless, let’s praise her!" highlights that India is incomparable, unique, and supreme in natural splendour, valour, and spiritual wisdom. The repetition (refrain) instils patriotic fervor, reinforces reverence for the motherland, and establishes a rhythmic musicality throughout the poem.
Marking Breakdown: [1 Mark] Content. [1 Mark] Language accuracy.
Q5. Environmental & Craft Value of Fans (Winds of Change)
Key Value Points:
Handcrafted fans (pankhas/bijnan) use natural, biodegradable materials like palm leaves, bamboo, Moonj grass, and cotton cloth without consuming electricity. They represent generations of indigenous artistic heritage, empowering rural craftspersons and preserving regional identity.
Marking Breakdown: [1 Mark] Content. [1 Mark] Proper sentence structure.
Q6. Dignity of Labour in "Gifts of Grace"
Key Value Points:
The poem pays equal tribute to all working hands—masons, carpenters, electricians, boatmen, shoemakers, cooks, and artisans. It asserts that every vocation has its own voice, rhythm, and dignity, collectively building the nation's fabric without hierarchy.

SECTION C: LONG ANSWER / COMPETENCY-BASED QUESTIONS (12 MARKS)

Q7. Prose Long Answer
Option (A): "For learning there is no age bar"
Context: Krishtakka was married young, lost her mother early, and remained illiterate due to traditional domestic duties, but had an unquenchable thirst for knowledge. Dedication: At 62, setting aside embarrassment, she took lessons from her 12-year-old granddaughter. She read, repeated, wrote, and recited slate exercises tirelessly. Reversal of Roles & Mutual Respect: The granddaughter became the respected Guru, and the grandmother became the obedient student. Climax: On Dussehra day, she touched the feet of her young granddaughter out of respect for the teacher and successfully read the novel Kashi Yatre independently.
Marking Distribution: [3 Marks] Content. [2 Marks] Fluency and paragraph structure. [1 Mark] Grammatical accuracy.

Option (B): Comparative Analysis — Sentila & Krishtakka
Sentila: Chose pottery over the socially expected craft of weaving. Despite her mother Arenla’s discouragement, she secretly learned clay preparation and pottery wheel dynamics through perseverance until her parents acknowledged her mastery. Krishtakka: Overcame the age barrier and societal assumptions that older women cannot learn to read. Refused to remain dependent on others to read stories. Common Thread: Both characters show unyielding internal motivation, defying conventional expectations to achieve personal growth and creative self-expression.
Marking Distribution: [3 Marks] Content. [2 Marks] Organisation. [1 Mark] Grammatical accuracy.
Q8. Poetry Long Answer
Option (A): Visual Imagery & Metaphor in "Canvas of Soil"
Core Concept: The poem establishes an extended metaphor equating soil to a painter's canvas and the gardener to an artist. Visual Imagery & Colour Palettes: "Palette of earth, rich and deep" – rich brown soil as the mixing board. "Brushstrokes of seeds" – precise, careful planting likened to brush movements. "Vibrant hue / Shades of green, red, and blue" – blossoming flowers representing rich paint strokes. Philosophical Insight: Art is not restricted to paint and paper; gardening is living art where life, patience, and aesthetics coincide in harmony.
Marking Distribution: [3 Marks] Content. [2 Marks] Coherence and analytical depth. [1 Mark] Grammatical accuracy.

Option (B): Contrast between "Bharat Our Land" & "Gifts of Grace"
"Bharat Our Land": Focuses on timeless macro-heritage: physical landmarks (Himavant, Ganga), ancient scriptures (Upanishads), sages, warriors, and philosophical truths (Buddha's Dhamma). Tone is epic, grand, and classical patriotism. "Gifts of Grace": Focuses on the dynamic micro-reality: everyday working-class citizens (electricians, carpenters, boatmen, weavers, shoemakers). Tone is democratic, celebrating the dignity of human labour that sustains modern society. Synthesis: While Bharati honours the spiritual and natural soul of India, Gifts of Grace honours the hands and hearts that build India daily.
Marking Distribution: [3 Marks] Content. [2 Marks] Reasoning and comparative transitions. [1 Mark] Grammatical precision.
