Q2.
In the poem 'Canvas of Soil', the garden serves as a powerful allegory, operating on a literal level as a physical space of cultivation and on a deeper level as a representation of human existence.

Firstly, the metaphor of 'brushstrokes of seeds' planted on a 'canvas of soil' compares the gardener to an artist and the act of gardening to painting. The 'canvas of soil' represents the blank slate of life or the world we are born into, while the 'seeds' symbolize our choices, actions, and potential. Just as seeds await 'spring's vibrant hue' to sprout, our efforts require time, patience, and the right conditions to bear fruit. This beautifully mirrors life's journey of growth, patience, and the cyclical nature of existence.

Secondly, the 'interaction of different colours' in the garden symbolises the beauty of diversity and harmony. A garden's aesthetic appeal lies not in a single, uniform color but in the coexistence of various plants, flowers, and shades. This serves as a symbolic reminder that our world is enriched by diverse cultures, identities, and perspectives. When these diverse elements interact peacefully, they create a harmonious and vibrant society, much like a well-tended, colorful garden.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Understanding of Allegory (1 Mark): The student must clearly identify the dual levels of meaning in the poem (the literal garden vs. the symbolic representation of life).
Analysis of 'Brushstrokes of seeds' and 'Canvas of soil' (2 Marks): The student must explain how these metaphors represent human agency, potential, patience, and the journey of growth in life.
Analysis of 'Interaction of colours' (1.5 Marks): The student must connect the variety of colors in the garden to the themes of cultural diversity, coexistence, and social harmony.
Clarity and Expression (0.5 Mark): The answer should be well-structured, coherent, and free of major grammatical errors, adhering to the expected word limit (~200 words).
