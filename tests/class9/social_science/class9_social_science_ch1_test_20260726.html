SECTION B: Short Answer Questions

Q6. Ancient Indian Concepts
Marking Scheme / Steps: Pañchamahābhūtas (1.5 Marks): Views human life as deeply embedded within nature (five elements). It parallels environmental geography by showing how natural surroundings shape settlements, occupations, and architecture.
Vasudhaiva Ku&turt;umbakam (1.5 Marks): Means 'the world is one family'. It reflects global interdependence, cross-cultural exchange, and peaceful co-existence—core principles studied in global history and international relations.
Q7. Epigraphic vs Numismatic Sources
Marking Scheme / Steps: Epigraphic Sources (1.5 Marks): Texts or decrees inscribed on durable surfaces like stone pillars, rock walls, or metal plates. Example: Brahmi pillar inscription of the Gupta period / Kannada inscription of Emperor Krishnadeva Raya at Hampi.
Numismatic Sources (1.5 Marks): Physical coins and currency medals. Example: Coin of King Samudragupta (4th century CE) / Mughal coin of Jahangir showing the Sagittarius zodiac sign.

SECTION D: Long Answer Questions

Q9. Social Science OR Historical Sources
Marking Scheme / Steps: (A) Definition of Social Science (1 Mark): The systematic study of human society, institutions, relationships, and human-environment interactions.
Four Disciplines & Focus (3 Marks - 0.75 Mark each):
Geography: Earth’s surface, physical features, climate, and spatial human-environment relationships.
History: Human past, continuity, change over time, and cultural evolution.
Political Science: Governance, power distribution, democratic institutions, rights, and civic duties.
Economics: Resource allocation, production, distribution, consumption, and economic development.
Civic Value & Empowerment (1 Mark): Helps citizens make informed choices, respect cultural diversity, question public issues logically, and participate in democratic governance.

--- OR ---

(B) Literary Sources: Manuscripts and written texts (e.g., Sāmaveda manuscript or Tamil Tirukku&rurt;a&lurt; palm-leaf text).
Archaeological Sources: Material remains, monuments, and artefacts (e.g., Terracotta figurines from Sindhu-Sarasvatī civilisation or 12th-century Vishnu sculpture).
Epigraphic Sources: Inscriptions carved on stone or metal (e.g., Brahmi pillar inscription of Gupta period).
Numismatic Sources: Ancient coins and currency (e.g., Samudragupta’s gold coins or Mughal Jahangir coins).
Q10. Governance and Grassroots Democracy
Marking Scheme / Steps: Ancient Political Thought (2 Marks): Early Indian thought linked political authority with Dharma (moral duty), Artha (economic well-being), and Rājadharma (duties of rulers toward public welfare), as detailed in the Arthaśāstra and Mahābhārata.
Modern Grassroots Democracy & Panchayati Raj (2 Marks): In modern India, the Panchayati Raj system decentralizes power to the village level, enabling citizens to participate directly in local development, decision-making, and public policy execution.
Role of Social Science (1 Mark): Social Science enables citizens to evaluate government accountability, understand how public policies affect daily life, and foster active civic participation.
