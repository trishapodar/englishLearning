SECTION B — 2 Mark(s) Each

Q6.   Focus vs Epicentre   [2 Mark(s)]

Focus (Hypocentre): The point inside the Earth's crust where an earthquake originates is called the focus. It is located below the surface.

Epicentre: The point on the surface of the Earth directly above the focus is called the epicentre. It experiences the maximum intensity of earthquake waves.

Marking: 1 mark for correct definition of focus. 1 mark for correct definition of epicentre.
Accept: Any correct distinguishing points between the two terms.
Common Error: Students often describe only one term or reverse their locations (above/below surface).

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q7.   Types of Igneous Rocks   [2 Mark(s)]

Extrusive igneous rocks: Formed when molten lava comes out on the Earth's surface, cools rapidly, and becomes solid. They have a fine-grained texture. Example: Basalt.

Intrusive igneous rocks: Formed when magma cools and solidifies deep inside the Earth's crust. They have a coarse-grained texture. Example: Granite.

Marking: ½ mark each for definition of extrusive and intrusive. ½ mark each for one correct example.
Accept: Extrusive: Basalt, Obsidian, Pumice. Intrusive: Granite, Gabbro, Diorite.
Common Error: Students confuse extrusive with intrusive or give sedimentary rock examples.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q8.   Earthquakes at Plate Boundaries   [2 Mark(s)]

Earthquakes are more common along plate boundaries because these are zones where tectonic plates interact with each other.

The plates move towards, away from, or alongside each other, causing stress to build up in the rocks. When this stress exceeds the strength of the rocks, it is released in the form of seismic waves, causing earthquakes.

Marking: 1 mark for stating plates interact/move at boundaries. 1 mark for explaining stress build-up and release.
Accept: Any reference to plate movement and release of energy at boundaries.
Common Error: Students give vague answers without mentioning plate movement or stress accumulation.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q9.   Types of Plate Boundaries   [2 Mark(s)]

The three types of plate boundaries are:

→ Convergent boundary (where plates move towards each other)

→ Divergent boundary (where plates move away from each other)

→ Transform boundary (where plates slide horizontally past each other)

Marking: ½ mark for each type correctly named; ½ mark for any brief description of movement.
Accept: Destructive/Constructive/Conservative boundaries as alternate terms.
Common Error: Students miss the transform boundary or confuse convergent and divergent.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

SECTION C — 3 Mark(s) Each

Q10.   Layers of Earth's Interior   [3 Mark(s)]

Crust: The outermost and thinnest layer. Continental crust is about 35 km thick (made of silica and alumina — SIAL). Oceanic crust is about 5 km thick (made of silica and magnesia — SIMA).

Mantle: Extends up to about 2,900 km below the crust. It is made of dense, semi-solid rock rich in iron and magnesium silicates. The upper portion of the mantle along with the crust forms the lithosphere.

Core: The innermost layer extending from about 2,900 km to 6,371 km. It is composed mainly of nickel and iron (NIFE). The outer core is liquid, while the inner core is solid due to extreme pressure.

Marking: 1 mark for each layer with correct composition and approximate thickness (total 3 marks).
Accept: Approximate thickness values; alternate terminology like SIAL, SIMA, NIFE.
Common Error: Students often miss the distinction between continental and oceanic crust, or confuse mantle and core compositions.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q11.   Rock Cycle   [3 Mark(s)]

The rock cycle is a continuous process through which rocks are transformed from one type to another:

→ Igneous rocks are formed from the cooling and solidification of magma/lava.

→ When igneous rocks are weathered and eroded, the sediments are deposited and compacted to form sedimentary rocks.

→ When any rock (igneous or sedimentary) is subjected to extreme heat and pressure, it transforms into metamorphic rocks.

→ When metamorphic rocks melt due to extreme heat, they become magma again, which can form new igneous rocks.

This continuous transformation is called the rock cycle.

Marking: 1 mark for explaining formation of each type. 1 mark for showing interrelation. 1 mark for mentioning the cyclic nature.
Accept: Labelled diagram showing the rock cycle with correct arrows and labels.
Common Error: Students draw incomplete cycles or miss the metamorphic-to-magma step.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q12.   Tectonic Plates and Earthquakes   [3 Mark(s)]

Tectonic plates are large fragments of the Earth's lithosphere (crust and upper mantle) that float over the semi-molten asthenosphere and are in continuous slow motion.

→ When two plates collide (convergent), one plate is forced under the other (subduction), building up stress.

→ When plates move apart (divergent), magma rises from below.

→ The stored energy from plate movement is suddenly released when rocks fracture along faults, producing seismic waves that we experience as earthquakes.

Marking: 1 mark for defining tectonic plates. 2 marks for explaining the mechanism of earthquake generation through plate movement.
Accept: Any clear explanation linking plate motion to energy release and earthquakes.
Common Error: Students define tectonic plates but fail to explain the mechanism of how movement leads to earthquakes.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q13.   Earthquake Safety Measures   [3 Mark(s)]

Safety measures during an earthquake:

→ If indoors: Take shelter under a sturdy table or desk (Drop, Cover, Hold On). Stay away from windows, heavy objects, and mirrors.

→ If outdoors: Move to an open area away from buildings, trees, and power lines.

→ After the earthquake: Do not use elevators. Check for gas leaks and injuries. Listen to emergency broadcasts for updates.

Marking: 1 mark for each valid safety measure (maximum 3 marks).
Accept: Any three valid, distinct earthquake safety precautions.
Common Error: Students list general safety tips not specific to earthquakes, or repeat the same measure in different words.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q14.   P-waves vs S-waves   [3 Mark(s)]

P-waves (Primary waves):

→ Travel fastest and arrive first.

→ Can travel through solids, liquids, and gases.

→ Are longitudinal waves (particles move in the same direction as wave propagation).

S-waves (Secondary waves):

→ Travel slower than P-waves and arrive second.

→ Can only travel through solid materials.

→ Are transverse waves (particles move perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation).

Marking: 1 mark for each correct point of distinction (maximum 3 marks).
Accept: Tabular format with at least three correct distinctions.
Common Error: Students confuse the mediums through which P-waves and S-waves travel.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q15.   Volcano Classification   [3 Mark(s)]

A volcano is a vent or opening in the Earth's crust through which molten rock (magma), gases, and ash escape to the surface.

Classification based on frequency of eruption:

→ Active volcanoes: Erupt frequently. Example: Mount Etna (Italy), Barren Island (India).

→ Dormant volcanoes: Have not erupted in recent history but may erupt again. Example: Mount Kilimanjaro (Tanzania), Mount Fujiyama (Japan).

→ Extinct volcanoes: Have not erupted in recorded history and are unlikely to erupt again. Example: Mount Kenya (Kenya).

Marking: 1 mark for defining volcano. ½ mark each for naming each type with example (total 2 marks for classification).
Accept: Any correct examples for each type of volcano.
Common Error: Students miss examples or confuse dormant with extinct volcanoes.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

SECTION D — 5 Mark(s) Each

Q16.   Plate Tectonics Theory   [5 Mark(s)]

Theory of Plate Tectonics:

According to this theory, the Earth's lithosphere is broken into several large and small rigid plates that float over the semi-molten asthenosphere. These plates are in constant slow motion due to convection currents in the mantle.

Types of plate movements:

→ Convergent (Destructive): Plates move towards each other. This leads to the formation of fold mountains, deep ocean trenches, and volcanic arcs. Example: The Himalayas formed due to the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.

→ Divergent (Constructive): Plates move apart. Magma rises to fill the gap, forming new crust. Example: Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

→ Transform (Conservative): Plates slide past each other horizontally. Example: San Andreas Fault.

Distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes:

Most earthquakes and volcanoes are concentrated along plate boundaries:

→ The Pacific Ring of Fire (circum-Pacific belt) accounts for about 68% of all earthquakes and most active volcanoes, located along convergent and transform boundaries around the Pacific Plate.

→ The Mid-Continental Belt (Alpine-Himalayan belt) runs through the Mediterranean to the Himalayas along convergent boundaries.

→ The Mid-Atlantic Ridge represents a divergent boundary where new oceanic crust is being created.

Marking: 2 marks for explaining the theory. 1 mark each for describing three types of plate movements. 2 marks for explaining distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes (with reference to major belts).
Accept: Labelled diagram showing major tectonic plates. Any clear explanation linking plate boundaries to seismic/volcanic activity.
Common Error: Students describe plate movements but fail to connect them to earthquake/volcano distribution patterns. Some miss the Pacific Ring of Fire.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2

Q17.   Types of Rocks and their Importance   [5 Mark(s)]

Rocks are classified into three main types based on their mode of formation:

1. Igneous Rocks (Primary Rocks):

→ Formed by the cooling and solidification of magma (below surface) or lava (on surface).

→ Extrusive: Basalt, Pumice. Intrusive: Granite, Gabbro.

2. Sedimentary Rocks:

→ Formed by the deposition, compaction, and cementation of sediments derived from the weathering and erosion of pre-existing rocks.

→ Examples: Sandstone, Limestone.

3. Metamorphic Rocks:

→ Formed when existing rocks (igneous or sedimentary) are subjected to extreme heat and pressure, leading to changes in their mineral composition and texture.

→ Examples: Marble (from limestone), Slate (from shale).

Importance for construction and civil engineering:

→ Granite (igneous) is extremely hard and durable — widely used for building foundations, bridges, and monuments.

→ Marble (metamorphic) is used for decorative flooring and sculptures due to its aesthetic quality.

→ Sandstone and limestone (sedimentary) are used for walls and road construction.

→ Knowledge of rock types helps engineers select appropriate materials, assess ground stability, and plan safe structures in different geological settings.

Marking: 1 mark each for explaining the formation of each rock type (3 marks). 1 mark for giving correct examples (at least 2 each). 1 mark for explaining importance in construction/engineering.
Accept: Any correct examples and valid applications in construction.
Common Error: Students describe rock types but fail to answer the application part about construction. Some confuse metamorphic examples.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Contemporary India-I, Ch. 2
