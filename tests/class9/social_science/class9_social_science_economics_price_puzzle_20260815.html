Section A

Q1. (b) The demand for tea will increase.
Explanation: Coffee and tea are substitute goods. When the price of coffee rises, coffee becomes relatively more expensive, prompting consumers to switch to tea, which increases the demand for tea.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for option (b).
Q2. (c) Excess Demand (Shortage)
Explanation: When buyers want to purchase more units than sellers are offering at a given price (Quantity Demanded > Quantity Supplied), a condition of Excess Demand (Shortage) exists.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for option (c).
Q3. (b) Guarantee a price floor that protects farmers from distress selling during bumper harvests.
Explanation: Minimum Support Price (MSP) acts as a price floor set above the free-market crash price to guarantee fair returns to farmers.
Marking Scheme: [1 Mark] for option (b).
Q4. (a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Section B

Q5. Law of Demand and Determinants
Model Answer & Marking Scheme: (a) Law of Demand: Other factors remaining constant (ceteris paribus), as the price of a good increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and as the price falls, quantity demanded increases (an inverse relationship). [1 Mark]
(b) Non-Price Determinants of Demand (Any two): Consumer income levels. [0.5 Mark]. Consumer tastes, preferences, and fashion trends. [0.5 Mark]. (Also accept: Prices of related goods / Seasonal changes / Future price expectations).
Q6. Supply, Demand, and Perishability
Model Answer & Marking Scheme: Perishability & Storage Constraint: Vegetables are perishable and spoil quickly without refrigeration; vendors cannot store them overnight without loss. [0.5 Mark]
Shift in Market Dynamics: By late evening, the footfall of morning buyers drops (demand decreases), while vendors are eager to clear remaining inventory before it rots (supply pressure). [0.5 Mark]
Price Adjustment: To induce buyers to purchase the remaining stock, vendors lower prices, moving down the demand curve to clear excess supply. [1 Mark]

Section C

Q7. Market Equilibrium and Data Interpretation
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Marks: (a) Definition of Market Equilibrium: Market Equilibrium is the state where the quantity demanded by consumers equals the quantity supplied by producers at a specific price (Demand = Supply), leaving neither shortage nor surplus. [1 Mark]
(b)(i) Equilibrium Identification: Equilibrium Price: ₹80 per kg [0.5 Mark]. Equilibrium Quantity: 70 kg [0.5 Mark]
(b)(ii) Price Adjustment at ₹120 per kg: At ₹120 per kg, Quantity Supplied (120 kg) > Quantity Demanded (20 kg), creating an Excess Supply (Surplus) of 100 kg. [0.5 Mark]. To sell their unsold stock, sellers will compete by lowering prices until the price falls back to the equilibrium level of ₹80 per kg. [0.5 Mark]
Q8. Price Ceiling vs. Price Floor (MSP)
Model Answer & Comparison: Price Ceiling: Legal maximum price set by the government below the equilibrium price. [0.5 Mark]. Beneficiary: Protects low-income consumers from price gouging. [0.5 Mark]. Example: Price caps on life-saving medicines, LPG cylinders, or ration grain. [0.5 Mark]
Price Floor / MSP: Legal minimum price set by the government above the equilibrium price. [0.5 Mark]. Beneficiary: Protects producers/farmers from income collapse. [0.5 Mark]. Example: MSP for wheat, rice, sugarcane, or minimum wage laws. [0.5 Mark]

Section D

Q9. Case-Based Model Answers
Case-Based Model Answers: Part (a) Supply Shock & Price Rise [1 Mark]: Heavy rains destroyed crops and blocked roads, causing a sharp drop in market supply (a leftward shift of the supply curve). At the initial price of ₹25, there was severe excess demand (shortage). Competition among buyers pushed the price up to ₹90 until supply and demand balanced at a higher equilibrium. [1 Mark]

Part (b) Hoarding & Buffer Stocks [2 Marks]: Effect of Hoarding: Speculative traders hide stock from the market to create artificial scarcity, shifting the supply curve further left and driving prices higher for unfair profits. [1 Mark]. Effect of Buffer Stocks: When the government releases stored onions into the market at ₹35, it directly injects additional supply, eliminating artificial shortage and pulling market prices down towards affordable levels. [1 Mark]

Part (c)(i) Substitute vs. Complementary Goods [2 Marks]: Substitute Goods: Goods that can be used in place of each other (e.g., Tea and Coffee, Coke and Pepsi). An increase in the price of one increases the demand for the other. [1 Mark]. Complementary Goods: Goods that are used together to satisfy a single want (e.g., Car and Petrol, Pen and Ink, Smartphone and SIM Card). An increase in the price of one decreases the demand for the other. [1 Mark]

Part (c)(ii) Complementary Goods Application [1 Mark]: Since back covers and mobile data are complementary goods to smartphones, a drop in smartphone prices will increase smartphone sales, which in turn will increase the demand for back covers and mobile data services. [1 Mark]
