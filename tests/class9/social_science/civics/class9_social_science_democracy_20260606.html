Q6.   Handling Conflicts   [2 Mark(s)]

Democracy provides a method to deal with differences and conflicts peacefully. In a diverse society, conflicts are inevitable. Dictatorships often suppress these differences by force, leading to resentment and instability. Democracy, however, allows different groups to voice their opinions and reach a consensus, preventing any one group from permanently dominating the others.

Marking: 1 mark for mentioning peaceful resolution/consensus. 1 mark for contrasting with dictatorship's use of force/suppression.
Accept: Any explanation highlighting dialogue and equal rights in democracy vs force in dictatorship.
Common Error: Students may give a generic 'democracy is good' answer without specifically addressing the 'differences and conflicts' aspect.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q7.   Universal Adult Franchise   [2 Mark(s)]

Universal Adult Franchise means that all adult citizens (usually 18 years and above) have the right to vote, regardless of their wealth, caste, race, gender, or religion. It is significant because it upholds the democratic principle of political equality ('one person, one vote, one value') and ensures that the government is chosen by a broad representation of the society.

Marking: 1 mark for defining Universal Adult Franchise. 1 mark for explaining its significance (political equality/representation).
Accept: Equal voting rights / Inclusive democracy.
Common Error: Students define it but fail to explain *why* it is significant.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q8.   Role of Parliament   [2 Mark(s)]

The Parliament plays a crucial role in India's democracy by:

1. Making new laws, changing existing laws, or abolishing old ones for the entire country.

2. Exercising control over those who run the government (the executive cannot take major decisions without Parliament's support) and controlling public money.

Marking: 1 mark for law-making role. 1 mark for executive control/financial control.
Accept: Legislative function / Checking the government's power / Forum for national debate.
Common Error: Students confuse the role of Parliament (legislature) with the Supreme Court (judiciary).

SECTION C — 3 Mark(s) Each

Q9.   Arguments for Democracy   [3 Mark(s)]

Three arguments supporting democracy are:

1. More accountable form of government: A democratic government responds to the needs of the people because it is accountable to the citizens.

2. Improves the quality of decision-making: Democracy is based on consultation and discussion, involving many people and meetings, which reduces the chances of rash or irresponsible decisions.

3. Enhances the dignity of citizens: Democracy is based on the principle of political equality, recognizing that the poorest and least educated have the same status as the rich and educated.

Marking: 1 mark for each valid argument explained clearly.
Accept: Allows room to correct mistakes / Deals peacefully with conflicts.
Common Error: Students list the features of democracy (e.g., 'elections are held') instead of arguments *for* it (why it's good).

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q10.   Democracy in South Africa   [3 Mark(s)]

The transition to democracy in South Africa involved:

1. Struggle against Apartheid: The African National Congress (ANC) led a long, intense struggle against the oppressive, racially discriminatory Apartheid regime imposed by the white minority.

2. Negotiation and Compromise: Realizing they could no longer rule through repression, the white regime released Nelson Mandela and began negotiations. Both the oppressors and the oppressed agreed to sit together to draw up a common constitution.

3. A New Constitution: After two years of debate, they created a democratic constitution that gave its citizens extensive rights, emphasizing equality for all races and genders, and moving from a state of conflict to a united democratic nation.

Marking: 1 mark for mentioning the struggle against Apartheid. 1 mark for the negotiation phase. 1 mark for the resulting inclusive democratic constitution.
Accept: Mention of Nelson Mandela's role and the ending of racial segregation leading to majority rule.
Common Error: Students focus only on what Apartheid was, without explaining the *transition* to democracy and the constitution-making process.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q11.   Conditions for Democratic Elections   [3 Mark(s)]

Three conditions that make an election democratic are:

1. Universal Adult Franchise: Everyone should have one vote and every vote should have equal value, meaning everyone should be able to choose.

2. Real Choice: There should be something to choose from. Parties and candidates should be free to contest elections and offer a real choice to the voters.

3. Regular Elections: The choice should be offered at regular intervals; elections must be held regularly after every few years.

Marking: 1 mark for each condition correctly explained.
Accept: Elections conducted in a free and fair manner / The preferred candidate should get elected.
Common Error: Students simply state 'people vote' without detailing the specific systemic conditions required.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q12.   Role of Judiciary   [3 Mark(s)]

The Judiciary plays a critical role in safeguarding democratic rights by:

1. Independence: It acts independently of the legislature and executive, ensuring fair judgments.

2. Judicial Review: The Supreme Court and High Courts have the power to interpret the Constitution and can declare invalid any law or executive action that violates fundamental rights or the Constitution.

3. Protector of Fundamental Rights: Citizens can approach the courts directly if their fundamental rights are violated, ensuring that the government does not abuse its power and acts within constitutional limits.

Marking: 1 mark for mentioning independence. 1 mark for judicial review/interpreting the constitution. 1 mark for protecting fundamental rights.
Accept: Acts as the guardian of the Constitution / Resolves disputes between citizens and government.
Common Error: Students confuse the judiciary's role with that of the police or legislature.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q13.   Myanmar and Military Rule   [3 Mark(s)]

Military rule in Myanmar contradicts democratic principles because:

1. Lack of Elected Rulers: The military seized power through force, not through democratic elections. The people did not choose the army rulers, violating the principle that final decision-making power must rest with elected representatives.

2. Suppression of Rights: Under military rule, fundamental democratic rights like freedom of speech, assembly, and political participation are severely restricted or denied. Political opponents (like Aung San Suu Kyi) are often imprisoned.

3. No Accountability: The military government is not accountable to the citizens. People have no peaceful means (like elections) to change the government if they are dissatisfied with its policies.

Marking: 1 mark for lack of elected rulers. 1 mark for suppression of rights/freedoms. 1 mark for lack of accountability to the people.
Accept: Any valid points demonstrating the contrast between military dictatorship and democratic features.
Common Error: Students may not know the specific context of Myanmar and provide generic answers about dictatorships.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q14.   Importance of Rights in Democracy   [3 Mark(s)]

Rights are essential in a democracy because:

1. Protecting Minorities: Rights protect minorities from the oppression of the majority, ensuring that the democratic rule of the majority does not become tyrannical.

2. Enabling Democratic Participation: Rights like freedom of speech, expression, and association are necessary for citizens to express their opinions, form political parties, and take part in political activities.

3. Checks on Government Power: Rights place limits on the authority of the government. They act as guarantees that the government cannot infringe upon basic human freedoms, even if elected by a huge majority.

Marking: 1 mark for each valid reason explained.
Accept: Essential for leading a life of dignity / Allows citizens to vote and contest elections.
Common Error: Students list the fundamental rights (e.g., Right to Equality) instead of explaining *why* rights as a concept are essential for democracy to function.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q15.   Democracy vs Non-Democracy   [3 Mark(s)]

Democratic Government:

→ Rulers are elected by the people through free and fair elections.

→ It is ruled within limits set by constitutional law and citizens' rights.

→ People enjoy basic political rights and freedoms (speech, association).

Non-Democratic Government (e.g., Dictatorship/Monarchy):

→ Rulers are not elected by the people (they assume power through force or inheritance).

→ The ruler often holds absolute power, unchecked by a constitution.

→ Citizens generally do not have fundamental political rights or the freedom to criticize the government.

Marking: 1 mark for contrasting how rulers are chosen. 1 mark for contrasting constitutional limits. 1 mark for contrasting citizens' rights.
Accept: Tabular format highlighting differences in accountability, elections, and rights.
Common Error: Students provide points only for democracy and forget to state the contrasting points for non-democratic governments.

Q16.   Electoral System and Challenges   [5 Mark(s)]

System of Elections in India (Democratic Features):

India's elections are considered democratic because they are based on Universal Adult Franchise, allowing every adult citizen to vote. The country is divided into electoral constituencies to ensure representative distribution. Most importantly, elections are conducted by an independent and powerful Election Commission, ensuring they are free and fair.

Challenges faced by the Indian Electoral System:

1. Money Power: Candidates and parties with a lot of money may not be sure of their victory, but they enjoy a significant, unfair advantage over smaller parties and independent candidates.

2. Criminal Elements: In some parts of the country, candidates with criminal connections have been able to secure 'tickets' from major parties, pushing others out of the electoral race.

3. Dynastic Succession: Some political parties are dominated by families, making it difficult for ordinary workers to rise to the top, denying a level playing field.

4. Lack of Meaningful Choice: Very often, major parties are quite similar in their policies and practices, offering little real choice to ordinary citizens.

Marking: 2 marks for explaining the democratic features of the Indian electoral system. 1 mark each for three valid challenges (3 marks).
Accept: Mention of EVMs, Model Code of Conduct for system features. Voter bribery, caste/religion-based politics as challenges.
Common Error: Students only discuss the challenges and ignore the first part of the question asking to explain the system, losing 2 marks.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy

Q17.   Political vs Permanent Executive   [5 Mark(s)]

Composition and Functions of Political Executive:

→ Composition: It consists of leaders elected by the people for a specific period (usually 5 years). It includes the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers.

→ Functions: The political executive is responsible for framing government policies, taking major decisions, and running the overall administration of the country. They are answerable and accountable to the people and the Parliament for all the consequences of their decisions.

Distinction between Political and Permanent Executive:

1. Selection: Political executives are elected by the people. Permanent executives (civil servants/bureaucrats) are appointed on a long-term basis through competitive examinations.

2. Tenure: Political executives have a short, fixed tenure (they leave office when their party loses power). Permanent executives remain in office even when the ruling party changes, serving until retirement.

3. Power and Role: The political executive is more powerful because they represent the will of the people and take the final decisions. The permanent executive is expert in their field, assists the political executive in daily administration, and advises them, but does not take the final policy decisions.

Marking: 2 marks for composition and functions of the Political Executive. 3 marks for three clear points of distinction (selection, tenure, power/role).
Accept: Tabular difference. Mention of IAS/IPS officers as examples of permanent executive.
Common Error: Students confuse the executive with the legislature, or assert that the permanent executive is more powerful because they are more educated.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Democracy
