b) Drying the mango slices under the sun removes the moisture from them. Since microbes cannot grow or survive without water, drying stops them in their tracks and preserves the mangoes for a long time.
c) Adding mustard oil to the pickle creates a protective layer that stops air from getting into the jar. By keeping air away, it deprives the microbes of the oxygen they need to grow, keeping the pickle safe.
d) Storing fresh mangoes in the refrigerator keeps them at a very low temperature. The cold environment inside the fridge is too cold for microbes to grow and multiply actively, which significantly slows down the process of food spoilage.

Q3. 1. (a) For a short period of one week, refrigeration is the most suitable method. It keeps the tomatoes fresh and juicy for immediate use. (b) For a long period of one year, sun-drying is the best method. It turns the tomatoes into a dry form that can last the whole year without spoiling, whereas fresh tomatoes would rot in a fridge over such a long time.

How these methods target microbial needs:
Refrigeration targets temperature: It lowers the temperature, making it too cold for microbes to grow and multiply.
Sun-drying targets moisture: It removes water from the tomatoes. Since all microbes need water to survive, removing moisture stops them in their tracks.
If left in an open bowl at room temperature, the tomatoes will spoil very quickly. This is because the kitchen counter provides all three conditions that microbes need to grow rapidly: moisture (from the juicy tomato), air (from the open bowl), and a warm, suitable temperature.
Marking Scheme / Steps: [1 Mark] Correctly identifies refrigeration for short-term preservation (one week) and sun-drying for long-term preservation (one year).
[1.5 Marks] Evaluates refrigeration: Explains that it lowers the temperature to slow down microbial growth, keeping food fresh for a short time but not suitable for a whole year.
[1.5 Marks] Evaluates sun-drying: Explains that it removes moisture (water) completely, which is essential for microbial survival, thereby preserving the food for a very long duration.
[1 Mark] Explains that storing tomatoes at room temperature in an open bowl provides moisture, air, and warmth, leading to rapid microbial growth and spoilage.
