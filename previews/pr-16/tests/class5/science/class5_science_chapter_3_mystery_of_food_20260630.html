Section B

Q1.
The two main signs of food spoilage that Disha observed on her uttapam are:

A foul smell coming out of the tiffin box.
Coloured patches appearing on the surface of the uttapam.

These changes are caused by the growth of microbes (like fungi or bacteria). Microbes grow on food when they get moisture, air, and the right temperature, leading to food spoilage.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Award 1 mark for identifying the first sign of spoilage: 'foul smell' or 'bad odour'.
Award 1 mark for identifying the second sign of spoilage: 'coloured patches' on the food.
Award 1 mark for explaining the cause: growth of microbes due to presence of moisture, air, or suitable temperature.
Q2.
1. Black pepper is dried to remove moisture. Without moisture, microbes cannot grow and spoil the spice, allowing it to last longer. 2. Commercial packaging in airtight cans keeps air and microbes away from the soup. Since microbes need air to survive and grow, sealing the soup in airtight cans stops them in their tracks and prevents spoilage.
Marking Scheme / Steps: State that drying black pepper removes moisture, which stops the growth of microbes. (1 mark)
Explain that airtight cans keep air and microbes away from the food. (1 mark)
Conclude that removing moisture and air are effective ways to stop microbes from surviving and growing, thus preserving the food. (1 mark)
Q3.
Microbes need moisture and the right temperature to grow and spoil food. Butter contains moisture (water). Therefore, it must be refrigerated because cold temperatures slow down the growth of microbes. On the other hand, ghee is prepared by heating butter until all the water separates and evaporates. Since ghee has no moisture, microbes cannot grow in it, allowing it to be stored safely at room temperature without spoiling.
Marking Scheme / Steps: State that microbes need moisture and suitable temperatures to grow and spoil food. (1 mark)
Explain that butter contains moisture, so it requires cold temperatures (refrigeration) to slow down microbial growth. (1 mark)
Explain that ghee is prepared by heating butter to evaporate all water/moisture; without moisture, microbes cannot grow, so it does not need refrigeration. (1 mark)
Q4.
Parents give curd or buttermilk for an upset stomach because they contain small, useful microbes. When consumed, these microbes join the good microbes already present in our stomach to help in digestion. Another food item where microbes are useful is idli, where microbes from the air help make the idli batter rise and become fluffy.
Marking Scheme / Steps: [1 Mark] State that curd or buttermilk contains small, useful microbes.
[1 Mark] Explain that these microbes join the good microbes in our stomach to help with digestion.
[1 Mark] Name another food item, such as idli (or idli batter), where microbes help it rise.
Q5.
The sandwich has become spoiled due to the growth of harmful microbes, which caused the foul smell and coloured patches. If Rohan eats this spoiled food, he could suffer from health issues like an upset stomach, indigestion, or vomiting. If he gets an upset stomach, a simple home remedy is to eat curd or drink buttermilk. These contain good microbes that help his body digest food and recover.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Identify that the food is spoiled by microbes, as shown by the foul smell and coloured patches. (1 Mark)
State the health effects of eating spoiled food, such as indigestion, vomiting, or an upset stomach. (1 Mark)
Suggest a home remedy like curd or buttermilk and explain that the good microbes in them help in digestion. (1 Mark)
