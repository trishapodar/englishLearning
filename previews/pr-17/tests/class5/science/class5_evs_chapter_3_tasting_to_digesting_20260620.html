Q1.
Our body signals its needs through various physical and emotional symptoms:

Symptoms of Hunger: Children may experience a headache, feel tired, get angry easily, or cry when they are hungry.
Symptoms of Dehydration/Weakness: When Nitu's body could not retain food and water due to vomiting and loose motions, she felt extremely weak, dizzy, and eventually fainted.

Mentioning any two symptoms of weakness/dehydration shown by Nitu (such as feeling weak, dizzy, or fainting): 1.5 Marks.
Q2.
Rohan could not taste the sugar because his tongue was dry. For us to taste any food, it must first mix and dissolve in the saliva in our mouth. The tongue has tiny bumps called taste buds. When the food dissolves in saliva, it flows over these taste buds, which then send signals to our brain to identify the taste. Without saliva, the sugar did not dissolve, so the taste buds could not detect its sweet taste.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Explanation that food needs to dissolve in saliva to be tasted. (1 mark)
Mention of taste buds (tiny bumps on the tongue) detecting the dissolved food and sending signals to the brain. (1 mark)
Application to Rohan's case: since his tongue was dry, the sugar did not dissolve, so the taste buds could not detect any taste. (1 mark)
Q3.
When a person is vomiting and has loose motions, their stomach is upset and cannot retain or digest any food or water. Drinking glucose water would not help as it would be vomited out. A glucose drip is given directly into the blood. This bypasses the stomach and provides instant strength and energy to the weak body immediately, even without eating.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Explain that during severe vomiting, the stomach is upset and cannot retain or digest food or liquids taken by mouth (1 mark).
Explain that a glucose drip is given directly into the bloodstream, bypassing the digestive system (1 mark).
State that this direct delivery provides instant strength and energy to the weak body immediately without the need for digestion (1 mark).
Q4.
'Proper food' refers to a balanced and nutritious diet that contains adequate amounts of food needed for a child's healthy growth and development. It includes fresh, home-cooked meals like roti, rice, dal, and vegetables, rather than junk food. It is important because without proper food, children can become weak, sickly, and frequently fall ill. Getting proper food is the basic right of every child to grow up healthy and strong.
Marking Scheme / Steps: Definition of proper food as adequate, fresh, and nutritious food needed for healthy growth (1 Mark).
Explanation of why it is important (prevents children from becoming weak, sickly, or undernourished) (1 Mark).
Mentioning that getting proper food is a fundamental right of every child to ensure healthy development (1 Mark).
Q5.
1. You can identify the lemon juice by its sharp sour taste using the taste buds on your tongue. 2. You can identify the fried fish even before tasting it by its strong, distinct smell using your nose. 3. Our sense of taste and sense of smell work together to help us identify flavors. Closing our nose blocks the smell, making it difficult to recognize the food fully.
Marking Scheme / Steps: [1 Mark] State that lemon juice is identified by its sour taste using the tongue.
[1 Mark] State that fried fish is identified by its smell using the nose.
[1 Mark] Explain that taste and smell work together to identify food, so closing the nose makes it difficult to recognize the flavor.
