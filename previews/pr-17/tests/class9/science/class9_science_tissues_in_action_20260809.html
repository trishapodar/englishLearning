Section B

Q5. Observation & Reason
Marking Scheme / Steps: (a) Observation: The roots of Bulb 'B' will stop growing in length after their tips are removed. [1 Mark]
(b) Scientific Reason: The root tips contain apical meristematic tissue, which consists of actively dividing cells responsible for longitudinal growth (growth in length). Once the root tips are cut off, the source of dividing cells is removed, preventing further cell division and root elongation. [1 Mark]
Q6. Tendon vs Ligament
Marking Scheme / Steps: (i) Connection:
Tendon: Connects skeletal muscle to bone [0.5 Mark]
Ligament: Connects bone to bone [0.5 Mark]
(ii) Nature of Matrix:
Tendon: Tough, fibrous tissue with high tensile strength and limited flexibility [0.5 Mark]
Ligament: Elastic tissue with high flexibility and considerable strength [0.5 Mark]
Q7. Xylem vs Phloem
Marking Scheme / Steps: (i) Direction of transport:
Xylem: Unidirectional (upward transport of water and dissolved minerals from roots to aerial parts). [0.5 Mark]
Phloem: Bidirectional / Multidirectional (translocation of food/organic solutes from source leaves to storage and growing regions). [0.5 Mark]
(ii) Living/Dead Nature of Conducting Elements:
Xylem: The main conducting elements (vessels and tracheids) are dead cells with lignified thick walls at maturity. [0.5 Mark]
Phloem: The main conducting elements (sieve tubes and companion cells) are living cells. [0.5 Mark]

Section C

Q8. Parenchyma, Collenchyma, and Sclerenchyma
Marking Scheme / Steps: (i) Living status at maturity: Parenchyma (Living cells with thin primary cell walls), Collenchyma (Living cells), Sclerenchyma (Dead cells devoid of protoplasm) [1 Mark total: 1/3 per tissue]
(ii) Cell wall thickening: Parenchyma (Thin cellulose cell walls with large intercellular spaces), Collenchyma (Irregularly thickened cell walls at corners due to pectin and cellulose; little intercellular space), Sclerenchyma (Uniformly and heavily thickened cell walls due to lignin deposition; no intercellular space) [1 Mark total: 1/3 per tissue]
(iii) Primary function: Parenchyma (Storage of food/nutrients, photosynthesis (chlorenchyma), and buoyancy (aerenchyma)), Collenchyma (Provides mechanical support and flexibility/elasticity (allows bending without breaking)), Sclerenchyma (Provides rigidity, hardness, and mechanical strength/protection (e.g., coconut husk)) [1 Mark total: 1/3 per tissue]
Q9. Nervous tissue
Marking Scheme / Steps: (a) Functions of Neuron Parts:
Dendrite: Receives incoming nerve impulses/chemical signals from sensory receptors or adjacent neurons and transmits them towards the cell body (cyton). [1 Mark]
Axon: Conducts the nerve impulse (electrical signal) away from the cell body towards the axon terminal/synapse. [1 Mark]
(b) Synapse & Transmission:
Definition: A synapse is a microscopic junction/gap between the axon terminal of one neuron and the dendrite of the adjacent neuron. [0.5 Mark]
Transmission Mechanism: When an electrical signal reaches the axon terminal, it triggers the release of chemical messengers (neurotransmitters), which diffuse across the synaptic cleft and generate a new electrical impulse in the dendrite of the next neuron. [0.5 Mark]

Section D

Q10. Human musculoskeletal system & Joints
Marking Scheme / Steps: (i) Joint Type: Ball and Socket Joint [0.5 Mark]
Example: Shoulder joint (pectoral girdle and humerus) OR Hip joint (pelvic girdle and femur). [0.5 Mark]
(ii) Joint Type: Hinge Joint [0.5 Mark]
Type of Movement: Allows angular movement restricted to a single plane (back-and-forth movement like a door on its hinges). [0.5 Mark]
(iii) Explanation: Ligaments are dense fibrous connective tissues that have a very limited / poor blood supply (low vascularity) compared to skeletal muscle tissue, which is richly supplied with blood capillaries. [1 Mark]
Because healing requires nutrients, oxygen, and cellular repair factors delivered via the bloodstream, the lower blood supply in ligaments causes them to heal much slower than vascularized muscles. [1 Mark]

(iii) Primary Function of Cartilage at Joints: It cushions the ends of bones at joints, smooths bone surfaces, prevents direct bone-to-bone friction, and absorbs mechanical shocks. [1 Mark]
