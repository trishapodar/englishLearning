SECTION B: VERY SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (6 MARKS)

Q7. Weather vs. Climate
Model Answer & Marking (2 Marks):
Weather: The state of the atmosphere at a particular place over a short period of time (e.g., day-to-day, hour-to-hour fluctuations in temperature, rainfall, or wind). [1 Mark]
Climate: The sum total of weather conditions and variations over a large area over a long period of time (usually 30 years or more). [1 Mark]
Q8. Need for Reserved Constituencies
Model Answer & Marking (2 Marks):
In an open electoral competition, weaker sections (SCs and STs) may lack the required resources, education, or contacts to win against powerful candidates. [1 Mark]
Reserved constituencies ensure that our representative democracy provides a fair voice and equal legislative representation to marginalized communities in Parliament and State Assemblies. [1 Mark]
Q9. Fixed Capital vs. Working Capital
Model Answer & Marking (2 Marks):
Fixed Capital: Assets used over many production cycles for years without being consumed immediately. Example: Tractors, factory buildings, sewing machines, computers. [1 Mark]
Working Capital: Raw materials and cash in hand that get used up or converted during a single production cycle. Example: Yarn for weavers, clay for potters, cash to pay wages. [1 Mark]

SECTION C: SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (9 MARKS)

Q10. Harappan Urban Planning & Drainage System
Model Answer & Marking (3 Marks): (1 Mark per distinct point, Max 3 Marks)
Grid-Based Town Layout: Cities were divided into two main parts—the elevated Citadel (public buildings) and the Lower Town (residential units). Streets were laid out on a grid pattern cutting at right angles. [1 Mark]
Covered & Underground Drains: Every house had its own drain connected to the street drain. Drains were constructed of baked bricks and covered with stone slabs or bricks. [1 Mark]
Inspection Holes & Sanitary Maintenance: Street drains were laid at gentle slopes and had inspection chambers (manholes) at regular intervals for regular cleaning and desilting. [1 Mark]
Q11. Role & Powers of the Election Commission of India
Model Answer & Marking (3 Marks): (1 Mark for each valid power, Max 3 Marks)
Control and Conduct: ECI takes decisions on all aspects of election conduct, from the notification of election dates to the declaration of final results. [1 Mark]
Enforcement of Model Code of Conduct: ECI enforces the Model Code of Conduct and can penalise/disqualify any candidate or political party that violates rules (e.g., bribing voters, using religious places for campaigning). [1 Mark]
Control over Government Machinery: During election duty, government officials work under the direct administrative control and supervision of the ECI, not the ruling political party. [1 Mark]
Q12. Market Price Determination & Excess Supply
Model Answer & Marking (3 Marks):
Equilibrium Price: Market price is determined at the point of intersection where the quantity demanded by buyers equals the quantity supplied by sellers (Market Equilibrium: Demand = Supply). [1.5 Marks]
Excess Supply (Surplus): If the market price is higher than the equilibrium price, supply exceeds demand. Sellers cannot sell all their stock, leading to competition among sellers to lower prices until the market reaches equilibrium again. [1.5 Marks]

Q13. Economics Case Study
Model Answer & Marking (4 Marks):

(ii) Two Factors of Production [1 Mark]: Any two of: Land, Labour, Physical Capital, or Enterprise/Organisation (0.5 Mark each). [1 Mark]

Q14. Long Answer
Option (A): River Landforms & Geomorphic Processes [5 Marks]
(i) Sequential Landforms of a River [3 Marks] (0.75 Mark per stage/landform): Upper/Mountain Course: Steep gradient causes deep vertical erosion forming steep V-shaped valleys, gorges, and waterfalls. Middle Course: As the river enters plains, lateral erosion causes broad curving loops called meanders. Cut-off Stage: Due to continuous erosion and deposition, meander loops narrow and get cut off from the main river, forming crescent-shaped Oxbow lakes. Lower/Coastal Course: Near the sea, the river slows down, splits into distributaries, and deposits sediments to form a fertile Delta.
(ii) Endogenic vs. Exogenic Processes [2 Marks]: Endogenic Forces: Forces operating from inside the Earth (e.g., earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, plate tectonics) that elevate or deform crustal relief. [1 Mark]. Exogenic Forces: Forces operating on the Earth’s surface driven by solar energy and gravity (e.g., weathering, erosion, running water, glaciers, wind) that wear down and level the landscape. [1 Mark]

Option (B): Critical Evaluation of Democracy [5 Marks]
(i) Arguments in Favour of Democracy [3 Marks] (1 Mark per point): More Accountable Form of Government: Rulers are accountable to the citizens and can be voted out of office in regular elections. Improves the Quality of Decision-Making: Democracy involves widespread consultations, discussions, and debates, reducing the likelihood of rash or irresponsible decisions. Enhances the Dignity of Citizens: Based on political equality (one person, one vote, one value), granting equal status to the richest and the poorest alike. Provides a Peaceful Method to Resolve Conflicts: Accommodates diverse social, religious, and linguistic groups without violence.
(ii) Arguments Against Democracy / Limitations [2 Marks] (1 Mark per point): Instability: Frequent changes in political leadership can disrupt long-term policies and governance continuity. Delays in Decision-Making: Because many people have to be consulted, democratic processes can be slow and cumbersome. Political Competition and Corruption: Focus on electoral victory often leads to unethical practices, use of money, and muscle power.
