SECTION B — 2 Mark(s) Each

Q6.   Endogenic vs Exogenic Forces   [2 Mark(s)]

Endogenic forces: Forces originating from within the Earth that build up or modify the surface. Example: Earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, folding, faulting.

Exogenic forces: Forces originating from the Earth's surface or atmosphere that wear down the surface. Example: Weathering, erosion by rivers, wind, or glaciers.

Marking: 1 mark for each correct definition with example.
Accept: Any valid endogenic example (earthquake, volcano, folding, faulting) and exogenic example (weathering, erosion, deposition).
Common Error: Students confuse the two or fail to give examples.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q7.   Meander and Oxbow Lake   [2 Mark(s)]

Meander: A large bend or loop formed by a river as it flows through its middle or lower course, due to lateral erosion on the outer bank and deposition on the inner bank.

Oxbow Lake formation: Over time, the meander loop becomes so pronounced that the river cuts through the narrow neck of the loop during a flood, creating a straight channel. The abandoned loop gets cut off from the main river and forms a crescent-shaped lake called an oxbow lake.

Marking: 1 mark for defining meander. 1 mark for explaining oxbow lake formation.
Accept: Any correct explanation of meandering and cut-off leading to oxbow lake.
Common Error: Students define meander but cannot explain the cut-off process that forms the oxbow lake.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q8.   Types of Weathering   [2 Mark(s)]

1. Physical (Mechanical) Weathering: Caused by temperature changes, frost action, or pressure release that physically break rocks apart.

2. Chemical Weathering: Caused by chemical reactions such as oxidation, carbonation, or hydration that decompose the mineral composition of rocks.

3. Biological Weathering: Caused by the action of living organisms — plant roots grow into rock cracks, burrowing animals loosen soil, lichens secrete acids.

Marking: ½ mark for naming each type. ½ mark for giving a correct cause. (Total 2 marks — ⅓ mark may be rounded.)
Accept: Any valid cause for each type. Frost action, thermal expansion for physical; oxidation, carbonation for chemical; root growth, burrowing for biological.
Common Error: Students list only two types or confuse chemical with biological weathering.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

SECTION C — 3 Mark(s) Each

Q10.   Layers of Earth's Interior   [3 Mark(s)]

Crust: The outermost and thinnest layer. Continental crust is about 35 km thick (composed of silica and alumina — SIAL). Oceanic crust is about 5 km thick (composed of silica and magnesia — SIMA).

Mantle: Extends from below the crust to about 2,900 km depth. Made of dense, semi-solid rock rich in iron and magnesium silicates. The upper mantle along with the crust forms the lithosphere; below it lies the semi-molten asthenosphere.

Core: The innermost layer from about 2,900 km to 6,371 km. Composed mainly of nickel and iron (NIFE). The outer core is liquid; the inner core is solid due to extreme pressure.

Marking: 1 mark for each layer with correct composition and approximate thickness.
Accept: Approximate thickness values. Alternate terminology: SIAL, SIMA, NIFE.
Common Error: Students miss the distinction between continental and oceanic crust, or confuse mantle and core compositions.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q11.   Gradation   [3 Mark(s)]

Gradation is the process by which the Earth's surface is levelled through the combined action of degradation and aggradation.

Degradation: The wearing away of the Earth's surface by agents of erosion (rivers, wind, glaciers, sea waves). It lowers elevated areas like mountains and plateaus. Example: A river eroding a V-shaped valley.

Aggradation: The building up of the Earth's surface through deposition of eroded material. It fills low-lying areas. Example: A river depositing sediments to form a delta or flood plain.

Together, degradation and aggradation work to level the surface — high areas are worn down and low areas are filled up, tending towards a more uniform landscape over geological time.

Marking: 1 mark for defining gradation. 1 mark for explaining degradation with example. 1 mark for explaining aggradation with example.
Accept: Any correct examples of erosion (degradation) and deposition (aggradation).
Common Error: Students explain erosion and deposition but fail to connect them as parts of the gradation process.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q12.   Tectonic Plates and Fold Mountains   [3 Mark(s)]

Tectonic plates are large, rigid segments of the Earth's lithosphere (crust and upper mantle) that float on the semi-molten asthenosphere and are in continuous slow motion driven by convection currents in the mantle.

Fold mountain formation at convergent boundaries:

→ When two continental plates move towards each other (converge), the sedimentary rocks between them are compressed and squeezed.

→ This compression causes the rocks to buckle and fold upwards, forming fold mountains.

→ Example: The Himalayas were formed by the collision of the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate over millions of years.

Marking: 1 mark for defining tectonic plates. 2 marks for explaining fold mountain formation at convergent boundaries with example.
Accept: Any clear explanation linking plate convergence to folding/compression. Alps as an alternate example.
Common Error: Students define tectonic plates but fail to explain the folding mechanism or miss the example.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q13.   Wind Landforms   [3 Mark(s)]

Wind is a powerful agent of erosion and deposition in arid (desert) regions where there is little vegetation to hold the soil.

Erosional landforms:

→ Mushroom Rocks (Pedestal Rocks): Wind-blown sand erodes the lower part of an isolated rock more than the upper part (because sand grains travel close to the ground), creating a mushroom-shaped structure.

→ Deflation Hollows: Shallow depressions formed when wind blows away loose, fine-grained material from the surface.

Depositional landforms:

→ Sand Dunes: Mounds of sand deposited by wind. Types include barchans (crescent-shaped) and longitudinal dunes.

→ Loess: Fine silt deposited by wind far from its source, forming fertile plains (e.g., loess plains of China).

Marking: 1 mark for explaining wind as an agent in deserts. 1 mark for one erosional landform. 1 mark for one depositional landform.
Accept: Any two correct wind-formed landforms with brief descriptions.
Common Error: Students confuse wind landforms with river landforms or describe only sand dunes without mentioning erosional features.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q14.   Folding vs Faulting   [3 Mark(s)]

Folding: When compressive forces act on rock layers, they bend or buckle into wave-like folds (anticlines and synclines). This creates fold mountains. Example: Himalayas, Alps, Andes.

Faulting: When tensional forces exceed the elasticity of rocks, they crack and fracture along a plane called a fault. Blocks of rock may be displaced vertically or horizontally. This creates block mountains (horsts) and rift valleys (grabens). Example: Vosges and Black Forest (block mountains), Rhine Rift Valley.

Marking: 1 mark for explaining folding with mountain type. 1 mark for explaining faulting with mountain type. 1 mark for examples.
Accept: Any correct examples of fold and block mountains.
Common Error: Students describe both processes but fail to name the specific mountain types (fold vs block) or mix up examples.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q15.   GLOF   [3 Mark(s)]

GLOF (Glacial Lake Outburst Flood): A sudden and catastrophic release of water from a glacial lake when the moraine dam or ice dam holding the lake breaks, sending a massive flood of water, mud, and debris downstream.

Why it is a growing concern in the Himalayas:

→ Climate change: Rising global temperatures are causing Himalayan glaciers to melt faster, increasing the size and number of glacial lakes.

→ Unstable moraine dams: These natural dams made of loose sediment can rupture without warning, especially during earthquakes or heavy precipitation.

→ Downstream impact: GLOF floods travel at high speed through narrow valleys, destroying villages, roads, hydropower projects, and bridges. Thousands of people in states like Uttarakhand, Sikkim, and Himachal Pradesh live in GLOF-risk zones.

Marking: 1 mark for defining GLOF. 1 mark for linking it to climate change and glacier melting. 1 mark for explaining downstream risks in the Himalayan context.
Accept: Any valid explanation connecting glacier retreat, moraine instability, and flood risks.
Common Error: Students define GLOF but cannot explain why it is a growing concern or miss the climate change connection.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q16.   Rock Cycle   [3 Mark(s)]

The rock cycle is a continuous process through which rocks are transformed from one type to another:

→ Igneous rocks form from the cooling and solidification of magma (intrusive, e.g., granite) or lava (extrusive, e.g., basalt).

→ When igneous rocks are weathered and eroded, the sediments are transported, deposited, compacted, and cemented to form sedimentary rocks (e.g., sandstone, limestone).

→ When any rock is subjected to extreme heat and pressure, it transforms into metamorphic rocks (e.g., limestone → marble, shale → slate).

→ When metamorphic rocks melt due to extreme heat deep inside the Earth, they become magma again, which can solidify into new igneous rocks.

This continuous transformation is called the rock cycle.

Marking: 1 mark for explaining formation of each rock type. 1 mark for showing interrelation/transformation. 1 mark for cyclic nature and examples.
Accept: Labelled diagram with correct arrows and labels showing the cycle.
Common Error: Students draw incomplete cycles or miss the metamorphic-to-magma step.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

SECTION D — 5 Mark(s) Each

Q17.   River Landforms   [5 Mark(s)]

A river shapes different landforms at each stage of its course:

Upper Course (Mountain Stage):

→ The river flows swiftly down steep slopes with high erosive power (vertical erosion).

→ V-shaped Valleys: The river cuts downward into rock, creating a narrow, deep valley with a V-profile.

→ Waterfalls: Formed when a river flows over a layer of hard rock overlying soft rock. The soft rock erodes faster, creating a step over which water falls. Example: Jog Falls (India).

→ Gorges / Canyons: Very deep, narrow valleys cut by the river. Example: Grand Canyon (USA).

Middle Course (Plain Stage):

→ The river slows down and begins lateral (sideways) erosion.

→ Meanders: Large S-shaped bends formed due to erosion on the outer bank and deposition on the inner bank.

→ Oxbow Lakes: When meander loops are cut off from the main river during floods, they form crescent-shaped lakes.

→ Flood Plains: Flat areas on either side of the river formed by repeated flooding and deposition of fine sediments (alluvium).

Lower Course (Delta Stage):

→ The river flows very slowly and deposits most of its sediment load.

→ Delta: A triangular landform created by deposition of sediments at the river mouth where it meets the sea. Example: Sundarbans Delta (India).

→ Distributaries: The main river splits into smaller channels as it navigates through the deposited sediments.

Marking: 2 marks for upper course landforms. 2 marks for middle course landforms. 1 mark for lower course (delta/distributaries). Award examples within.
Accept: Labelled diagram showing river profile with landforms. Any correct Indian or world examples.
Common Error: Students describe landforms but fail to assign them to the correct course. Some miss oxbow lake formation or confuse gorge with canyon.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface

Q18.   Plate Tectonics and Disasters   [5 Mark(s)]

Theory of Plate Tectonics:

The Earth's lithosphere is broken into several large and small rigid plates that float on the semi-molten asthenosphere. These plates are in constant slow motion driven by convection currents in the mantle.

Types of plate boundaries and associated landforms:

→ Convergent (Destructive): Plates move towards each other. Continental collision creates fold mountains (e.g., Himalayas). Oceanic-continental convergence creates volcanic arcs and deep ocean trenches (e.g., Mariana Trench).

→ Divergent (Constructive): Plates move apart. Magma rises to fill the gap, forming new oceanic crust. Example: Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

→ Transform (Conservative): Plates slide horizontally past each other. Example: San Andreas Fault (California).

Earthquakes and Volcanoes:

→ Most earthquakes and volcanoes occur along plate boundaries. The Pacific Ring of Fire accounts for ~68% of earthquakes and most active volcanoes. The Alpine-Himalayan Belt is another major seismic zone.

Earthquake Preparedness:

→ If indoors: Drop, Cover, and Hold On under sturdy furniture. Stay away from windows and heavy objects.

→ If outdoors: Move to open ground away from buildings, trees, and power lines.

→ General: Keep an emergency kit ready. Know evacuation routes. Do not use elevators during an earthquake.

Marking: 2 marks for explaining plate tectonics theory and types of boundaries. 2 marks for earthquakes, volcanoes, and landform distribution. 1 mark for earthquake preparedness measures.
Accept: Labelled diagram of tectonic plates. Any valid preparedness measures.
Common Error: Students describe plate boundaries but fail to connect them to specific landforms or disaster distribution. Some skip the preparedness section.

Ref: NCERT Class 9 Gateway to Social Science, Ch. Shaping of the Earth's Surface
